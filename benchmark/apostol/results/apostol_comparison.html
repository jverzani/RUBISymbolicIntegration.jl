Symbolic integration · 175 problems from Apostol's Calculus

Apostol Integration Comparison

Four integrators on the apostol problems in test/problems_apostol.jl: RUBISymbolicIntegration's rules (on SymbolicUtils and on SimpleExpressions terms), RischIntegrate on SymbolicUtils terms, and SymbolicIntegration.jl's rule-based and Risch methods on Symbolics terms. Each answer was also checked by comparing a numerical derivative of the answer with the integrand.

What stands out

RischIntegrate solves the most. Every answer it gives that can be checked is right; it verifies its own answers by differentiating them.

SymbolicIntegration's rule-based method gives wrong answers for ∫ x² sin x, ∫ x³ cos x and ∫ x³ sin x (problems 23–25): 2(−cos x + x sin x) − x² cos x has the sign of 2 cos x reversed. The cause is rule 4_1_10_1_1, a hand-written rule for (c + dx)ᵐ cos(e + fx) that adds the remaining integral where it should subtract it. RUBISymbolicIntegration had the same rule and it is fixed there; SymbolicIntegration.jl 3.8.1 still has it.

RUBISymbolicIntegration went from 86 solved (77 verified) to 128 (125), ahead of SymbolicIntegration's rule-based method (124, 117 verified), after fixes including: the sign in rule 4_1_10_1_1; CommutativePatternMatching compared a SymbolicUtils term with == in different_powers, which threw and stopped the integration (33 problems); and the Symbolics extension built special functions on a placeholder x instead of their arguments. Of its 128 answers, 125 are verified; the other 3 have an incomplete gamma function, which stays unevaluated with SymbolicUtils, or sqrt(-1) as a constant. The 4 remaining errors are a DomainError from raising an integer to a negative power.

RUBISymbolicIntegration on SimpleExpressions solves 133, more than on SymbolicUtils (128) and more than SymbolicIntegration's rule-based method (124), with no wrong answers, and every one of its 133 answers checks out. Its special functions are those of SpecialFunctions.jl (expinti, gamma, loggamma), PolyLog.jl (reli) and HypergeometricFunctions.jl (_₂F₁), so the answers evaluate numerically. Most of its 6½ minutes is SimpleExpressions compiling: it encodes each expression's structure in its type, so each new shape compiles anew; rerun in the same session, problems take a fraction of a second.

Matching quotients and square roots: SymbolicUtils keeps 1/(x log x) as a quotient and SimpleExpressions keeps 1/√u as u^(-1/2); CommutativePatternMatching now matches both against rules written the other way (problems 64 and 105), and splits a quotient's denominator into factors for a product pattern (110, 126, 130). ext_expand no longer multiplies out a rational function's denominator, which lost the factors the partial-fraction rules need (110, 129), and when no rule applies the frontend tries both the simplified and the fully expanded integral (105, 111).

RischIntegrate depends on the Julia version for problem 165, ∫ eᵗ log(1 + t) dt: on Julia 1.12 it returns an answer with expinti, on Julia 1.13 (this run) it throws NotElementary.

How this was run

Solved means the method returned a result with no unevaluated ∫ (RischIntegrate throws when it does not integrate). An answer is verified when the central difference of the answer, at x = 0.31 and x = 0.57 with every parameter given a fixed value, matches the integrand to 1e-4. Answers that contain functions the term type cannot evaluate numerically are counted as solved but not checked; such an answer may still be wrong.

Each method ran in its own Julia process, one after another, with a limit of s per problem; after a timeout the process was restarted at the next problem. Integration time is the sum of the per-problem times from a cold start, so it includes compiling. Load time is the time to load the packages.
